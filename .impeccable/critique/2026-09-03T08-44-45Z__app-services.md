## Design Specificity Verdict

**Split. One page is authored; three are a category template with the nouns swapped.**

`/services/tv-walls-lighting-panels` is unambiguously authored for this business. The proof is in the nouns it commits to: NRC 0.85 acoustic felt, 3/4″ Baltic birch lagged into studs, 45° mitred waterfall corners, Häfele brackets, Lutron Caséta, 24V Class-2 drivers, 2700K, 90+ CRI, and the Ontario Electrical Safety Code prohibition on flexible cords in enclosed cavities (`:680`). No template generates that. It reads like a carpenter dictated it.

The three templated pages are category-interchangeable at the layout level and only partly rescued by their copy. The flooring intro's "most flooring and tile failures start in the subfloor" (`services.ts:236`) and the basement intro's "can't be insured properly, can't be declared when you sell, and can be ordered closed" (`:329`) are real, owner-voiced arguments. But they pour into a 7-section skeleton that would render identically for a plumber, a roofer, or a landscaper. The one gesture of authorship in the whole template is the hardcoded `service.slug === "legal-basements"` guide card (`[slug]:107-146`) — an escape hatch that proves the template couldn't express what the page needed.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json app/services` → `[]`, exit 0, on the directory and on each file individually. **No findings.** `npx tsc --noEmit` clean. The detector's silence is meaningful here: every defect below is a content, structure, or system-drift problem, not a pattern the linter models.

**Where the two assessments disagreed, and who was right:** the design review flagged `rgba(247,245,241,.5)` at 10.5px on the price block as failing AA at ~3.4:1. I computed it: blended over `#000` it is **4.97:1 — it passes**. The scan was right. Exactly one value on this surface fails: `rgba(247,245,241,.45)` at `tv-walls:282`, computing to **4.12:1**. Do not let a broad "low-opacity text" remediation loose on these files; there is one instance to fix.

**Visual overlays:** none. No browser-automation tool is exposed in this session, so no live server was started and no overlay exists in a browser tab.

## Overall Impression

The visual world is genuinely good and the placeholder governance is better than most shipped design systems — `placeholders.ts` registers every invented value with a `where` and a launch priority. Then the site ships 19 `TODO(owner):` strings as customer-facing copy and one fabricated case study with false alt text.

The single biggest opportunity is not a redesign. **It is that the TV walls page already proved what a service page on this site should be, and three services are still on the skeleton it outgrew.** That page answers *should I hire these people for this*. The other three answer *what is this service*. Only the first one sells.

## What's Working

1. **The `notIncluded` mechanism is a real competitive weapon.** Publishing exclusions before the quote conversation, with the source comment explaining why (`[slug]:165` — "stating exclusions up front prevents quote disputes"), separates this from a lead-gen farm. Kitchens' and flooring's lists are specific enough to actually reduce disputes.
2. **The ESA compliance block** (`tv-walls:617-685`) names the legal prohibition, names the certifying bodies, and closes on "safe, and insured" — turning a regulatory fact into a competitor-disqualifying claim. This is the template every service page needs: *the specific way we do this that the cheap guy doesn't.*
3. **The legal-basement guide card** (`[slug]:107-146`) — a named regulatory promise plus a 60-second self-qualification tool, offered before asking for anything. The best-converting element on the templated surface.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] 19 `TODO(owner):` strings render as customer-facing copy
`placeholders.ts:10` already declares the gate: *"`grep -rn "TODO(owner)" app/` must return nothing."* It currently returns 27 in `services.ts` alone. Of those, 4 are source comments and 5 are dead data on the TV route (the static segment wins over `[slug]`, so that entry's fields are never read) — **18 reach live copy**: kitchens 7, flooring 6, basements 6.

Worst instance: the **entire `notIncluded` array on legal-basements** (`services.ts:339-341`) is three unanswered questions, rendered under the eyebrow "Not included" as "— TODO(owner): who pulls the permit — you or the homeowner?" It carries no `DraftBlock` (only the price band is wrapped), and because `contentStatus: "complete"` the page's `DraftTag` never renders. The most damaging leak on the site is also the least marked, on the highest-liability page.

**Fix:** answer them or convert each to a supportable promise, following the `about.ts` precedent already set in this repo — move the question into a team-facing `needs` field, publish only what is true today. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P0] The "Glebe Media Wall" case study is fabricated
`tv-walls:945-1088` presents a featured project: "The Glebe · 4 Days on Site", three outcome bullets, and an image at `/assets/portfolio-wardrobe.jpg` with `alt="The Glebe Acoustic Slat Media Wall by SS Carpentry"` (`:1002`) — a wardrobe photo carrying a specific false factual claim. `projects.ts` holds two real projects, both `serviceSlug: "kitchens-bathrooms"`. There is no Glebe project. The section carries **no `DraftTag`** while the hero 900 lines above it does.

This violates the project's own written policy verbatim — `placeholders.ts:11-13`: *"ZERO STOCK HUMANS POLICY: No stock photograph may stand in for a real person, a real project, or a real credential. Text may be drafted; people and proof may not."*

Both of its CTAs (`:976`, `:1069`) point at `/gallery?service=tv-walls-lighting-panels`, which filters to zero matches and renders "No projects found in this category yet." The most confident CTAs on the best page lead to an empty state.

**Fix:** delete the section until a real project exists, or drive it from `getProjectsByService()` and render nothing when the array is empty. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] Zero proof on all four pages
No reviews, no project photos below the hero, no credentials, no warranty, no WSIB — on the four pages where the hire decision is made. `REVIEWS` is imported by `app/page.tsx:10`, `about/page.tsx:10`, and `contact/page.tsx:9`, and by **zero service pages**. Arman Sandhu's review says "complete work on our kitchen and tiles" — a written testimonial for two of the four service lines, sitting unused. `getProjectsByService()` exists at `projects.ts:122` and is called by nothing.

Two registered placeholders point at sections that were never built: `warranty-terms` → *"Service pages — guarantee block"*, and `payment-schedule` → *"Service pages — how quotes work"*.

**Fix:** service-filtered review strip + project strip + guarantee block, each rendering nothing when its data is empty. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] No inline quote form, and conversion is deferred five screens down
`QuoteForm` is well built — 5 fields, 3 required, honeypot, `aria-invalid`/`aria-describedby`/`aria-live` — and is mounted on exactly two pages, neither of them a service page. Every service page terminates persuasion in a link to a different route, where the service the visitor was just reading is **not pre-selected** (`defaultService` exists as a prop and is passed nowhere).

On the templated pages there are **zero CTAs above the fold**; the first and only conversion cluster is section 6 of 7. The strongest asset on the page — "We reply to every enquiry within one working day" — appears once, in section 4.

Underneath it: `sendQuoteLead.ts:38` falls back to `COMPANY.email` when `LEAD_TO_EMAIL`/`RESEND_API_KEY` are unset, and `lead-destination` is registered `blocks-launch`. Adding forms before that is resolved multiplies a funnel that may be discarding leads.

**Fix:** hero CTA pair on the templated route; embed `<QuoteForm source={slug} defaultService={service.title} />` above the cross-sell. **Suggested command:** `/impeccable onboard`

### [P2] Systemic drift from the canonical system — motion is the visible one
`DESIGN.md:3` names `app/page.tsx` as the design authority. Measured against it:
- **Zero `.reveal` / `.stagger-children` classes on either service route**, against 26 on the homepage. A visitor moving from a homepage where everything eases in on a 0.85s custom bezier hits a completely static document at the point of highest intent. Most visible to a human, cheapest to fix.
- **Zero use of `.hero-h1` / `.h2` / `.h2-xl`.** Both routes hand-roll local style objects: 4 distinct h1 scales and 3 h2 scales inside one route family, every one of them smaller than the homepage's 92px cap — so service pages read as a quieter tier of the same brand, the opposite of what a conversion page wants.
- **No Roman-numeral spine on either route.** The template's local `eyebrow` matches the canonical spec exactly (11px/.32em/GOLD) but is unnumbered; the TV page's is `.28em` and appears in **3 of 12 sections**, so the section rhythm simply stops.
- **32 hardcoded gold values** across the three files despite all three importing `GOLD`; `LightingToggle` alone adds 18 fresh near-black hexes.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable animate`, then `/impeccable typeset`

### [P2] Both hero sections keep 56px side padding on mobile
`globals.css:428-434` overrides `section` to `padding: 0 20px !important` at ≤768px, and `.hero-content` to `40px 20px 30px`. Both service heroes put their padding on a **bare inner div** (`[slug]:86` `"0 56px 64px"`, `tv-walls:191` `"0 56px 72px"`) carrying neither. `.hero-content` is used on `app/page.tsx:328` and nowhere else. On a 375px phone that consumes 112px, leaving ~263px for a `clamp(38px, 6vw, 74px)` serif H1. Both routes also hardcode `<div style={{ height: 88 }} />` under a nav that drops to 74px.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

## Persona Red Flags

**Priya — comparison shopper.** Three quotes for a $40k kitchen, three tabs open, scanning for a number and a photo. On `/services/kitchens-bathrooms` she finds `$00,000`, a scope list containing "TODO(owner): confirm — is plumbing rough-in and fixture installation handled in-house", and no photograph of finished work below the hero. She never reads the excellent intro paragraph. She closes the tab before reaching the CTA — and she is most of the traffic.

**Marc — risk-averse landlord.** His entire decision is *will this pass inspection and will my insurer honour it*. He lands on the one page that cannot tell him who pulls the permit or whether zoning is checked, because the answers are printed as questions. The guide card genuinely reassures him — then he finds no credentials, no WSIB clearance, no liability certificate, and no photograph of a completed suite. He calls a competitor.

**Dana — design-forward renovator.** The TV walls page is built for her and mostly works, until the emotional peak is a CSS diagram rather than a photograph, the price she was relieved to find is stamped "Placeholder" (`DraftBlock` wraps real numbers — `$3,500` / `$4,500–$9,500` / `$10,000+`), and both "see the projects" buttons deliver an empty state. She was ready to convert and the page ran out of evidence.

- `tv-walls:660` has an `<h3>` with no `<h2>` in its section; h3 sizes on that page run 16/21/24/28px — four visual weights on one heading level.
- `services.ts:7` instructs the next author to "match the depth" of legal-basements — which is the shortest and most TODO-riddled of the three templated entries.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the TV walls page is what a service page should be, why is it a separate file instead of a richer template? Every section on it — materials, engineering, compliance, tiered pricing, featured project — is a data shape the other three services also have. What would `services.ts` look like if it were designed around *that* page instead of around the 7-section skeleton?
2. Four Google reviews are displayed three times (home, about, contact) and zero times on the four pages where money is decided. Is the review section being treated as *branding* rather than as *evidence*?
3. The site publishes a placeholder governance system more rigorous than most shipped design systems — and then ships a fabricated case study with false alt text on its flagship page. What is the governance system for, if the one page nobody templated is also the one page nobody audited?
4. Everything on this site animates in except the pages that ask for the sale. Was that a decision, or did both service routes get written before `ScrollReveal` existed?
