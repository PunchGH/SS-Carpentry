## 2. Building Code requirements — from the province's second-unit guide

Source for this entire section: **[ontario.ca/page/add-second-unit-your-house](https://www.ontario.ca/page/add-second-unit-your-house)**, retrieved 2026-09-02.

### Ceiling height
| Requirement | Figure |
|---|---|
| Basement second unit | **1.95 m (6' 4¾")** over the entire required floor area, including the route to the exit |
| Attic second unit | ≥50% of required floor area at **2.03 m (6' 8")**; areas under **1.4 m (4' 7")** excluded from the calculation |

*Cited by the province as: Division B, Table 11.5.1.1.C., Compliance Alternative 102*

### Fire separation
| Requirement | Figure |
|---|---|
| Between units, and between units and common areas | **30 minutes** |
| Reduced rating **if the whole house has interconnected smoke alarms** | **15 minutes** |
| Example assembly given | 38×89 mm wood studs, 13 mm drywall both sides, fibre insulation |

*Cited as: Division B, Table 11.5.1.1.C., Compliance Alternatives 147, 152, 153*

### Escape / egress windows
| Location | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Basement / ground floor** | Min area **0.38 m² (4.1 sq ft)**; openable portion **≥460 mm (18")**; sill **≤900 mm (2'11")** above floor |
| **Upper floors** | Height **≥1,060 mm (3'6")**; width **≥560 mm (1'10")**; sill **≤900 mm (2'11")** |

*Cited as: Division B, Table 11.5.1.1.C., Compliance Alternative 136*

### Smoke alarms
Required: on every level of the house · outside sleeping areas · **in each bedroom in the second unit** · in common areas. Standard **CAN/ULC S531**, with flashing-light activation required.
*Cited as: Division B, Subsection 9.10.19*

### Carbon monoxide alarms
Required where the furnace burns natural gas, propane or similar fuels, **or** where there is an attached garage. Placement: near bedrooms/sleeping areas in the second unit, and in the furnace room if it is a separate space.
*Cited as: Division B, Articles 9.33.4.1 & 9.33.4.2, modified by Table 11.5.1.1.C., Compliance Alternative 197*

### Natural light (glazing)
Living/dining **5%** of floor area · bedrooms **2.5%** · kitchens, bathrooms, laundry not required.
*Cited as: Division B, Article 9.7.2.3, modified by Compliance Alternative 107*

### Electrical
Separate **electrical permit** required; **ESA inspection mandatory**. Electrical Safety Authority: 1-877-ESA-SAFE (372-7233).

## 3. Ottawa — zoning status ⚠️ this is the subtle one

Source: **[ottawa.ca — Zoning By-law 2026-50](https://ottawa.ca/en/living-ottawa/laws-licences-and-permits/laws/laws-z/zoning-law-law-no-2026-50)**, retrieved 2026-09-02.

- Zoning By-law **2026-50 enacted by Council March 11, 2026**
- **Applications deemed complete on/after Mar 11 2026 must comply with BOTH By-law 2008-250 AND 2026-50 — "the most restrictive provisions from both by-laws applying"**
- Applications deemed complete **before** Mar 11 2026 → 2008-250 only
- **July 2, 2026:** OLT oral decision allowed parts *not* under appeal to come into force, retroactive to Mar 11 2026. **The majority of 2026-50 is now in force — but 25 appeals remain outstanding.**
- Provisions still under appeal are being marked in **bold italic** in the online consolidation; site-specific appeals marked `*_` on the zoning map. Until that's complete, the [July 7 2026 memo to Council](https://ottawa.ca/en/city-hall/open-transparent-and-accountable-government/public-disclosure/memoranda-issued-members-council/memoranda-issued-planning-development-and-building-services/update-status-appeals-new-zoning-law-july-7-2026) is the authority on what is not in force
- New **Neighbourhood Zones N1–N6** (N5/N6 = mid- and high-rise)

**This dual-by-law "most restrictive applies" state is the single most valuable thing on the page.** It is genuinely confusing, actively current, and almost no competitor will have written about it. It also strongly justifies the feasibility-visit CTA.

## 4. Ottawa — permits and process

Source: **[ottawa.ca — Adding an apartment (additional dwelling units)](https://ottawa.ca/en/planning-development-and-construction/building-and-renovating/do-i-need-building-permit/adding-apartment-additional-dwelling-units)**, retrieved 2026-09-02.

- **Building permit required before proceeding.** Even with little or no physical work, a proposed **"Change of Use"** must be reviewed for Building Code and other applicable law compliance
- Lots on **municipal water + wastewater**: up to **two additional dwelling units** (e.g. two subsidiary apartments, or one apartment + a coach house)
- Lots on **private services (septic/well)**: limited to **one** additional dwelling or coach house — septic capacity to be confirmed with the Ottawa Septic Office
- Where **two** ADUs are added within the principal building, the designer must be registered under a **BCIN**. A homeowner may take design responsibility for **one** ADU where no other exists, or for a coach house
- New unit must be on the same lot and have **separate access on the ground floor**, unless building/fire codes allow alternate means
- **No additional parking space is required.** If one is provided it cannot be in the front yard; **tandem parking in the existing driveway is permitted**
- First review: **15 business days** (Council-approved enhanced service level)
- Inspections required at key stages; **book 48 hours in advance**; permit plans must be on site
- Applications submitted online via My ServiceOttawa / Land Management System
- **Pre-2005 exception:** SDUs were permitted before 2005 in Cumberland-Urban, Gloucester, Nepean-Urban, Osgoode, Goulbourn and West Carleton. Units existing before 2005 in those areas must comply with the Building Code and Fire Code
- Financial notes worth repeating on the page: MPAC may reassess **property taxes**; rent is income under the *Income Tax Act*; **notify your insurer** — policy may need adjusting

## 6. Open risks — do NOT publish these without further verification

1. **⚠️ Highest: the province's second-unit guide cites 2012-code section numbers** (Table 11.5.1.1.C compliance alternatives) while the **2024 code has been mandatory since Apr 1 2025**. The *figures* in §2 are the province's current published guidance, but the *citations* may be stale, and the 2024 code harmonized two-unit provisions with the NBC — so some figures may have moved. **Before publishing: verify §2 against the 2024 Code Compendium (Div B Part 9 / Part 11) or have the owner's BCIN designer confirm.** Publish the figures only once confirmed; cite the 2024 code, not 332/12.
2. That same ontario.ca page carries its own caveat: parts "may not accurately reflect recent changes to land use planning rules introduced under the *More Homes Built Faster Act, 2022*."
3. **Which zones permit ADUs, and at what size** — not verified. With 25 appeals live and two by-laws stacking, **the page must not tell anyone what their lot allows.** Point to the zoning map + Development Information Officer (613-580-2424 ext. 23434, dioinquiry@ottawa.ca) and to the feasibility visit.
4. **Permit fees** — not retrieved; ottawa.ca fee schedule is the source. Don't state a number.
5. **Overall project timeline** — only the 15-business-day *first review* is sourced. Don't state a total duration; that's still a `TODO(owner)` in `services.ts`.
6. **Unit size / 40% GFA rule** appeared only in search snippets from contractor blogs and was **not** verified on ottawa.ca. Treat as unverified — likely from the old by-law.
