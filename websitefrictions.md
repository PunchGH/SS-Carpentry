# Website Friction Guide — Home Remodelers

A field guide for auditing a remodeler's site the way their buyer experiences it.
Built for extensions, kitchens, bathrooms, and loft conversions.

**Confidence tags used throughout**
✅ proven — evidence found, safe to say on a call
🟡 likely — indirect or anecdotal support
🔵 my reasoning — do not state as fact to a prospect
⚠️ disputed — credible people disagree, ask rather than assert

---

## Before you start

**Set the persona.** Pick one project type and one buyer, and hold it for the whole audit.
Default: *side return extension · couple, first renovation, ~£70k budget, needs it done in a year.*

**Audit on your phone first.** That's how the buyer arrives. Desktop second.

**Time-box it to 10 minutes per site.** You are not doing design review. You are answering eight
questions on the buyer's behalf and noting where the site goes silent.

**The one rule.** For every check, ask *"could the buyer answer this without phoning anyone?"*
If no, that's friction. Grade it Answered / Partial / Absent — nothing else.

**Why this works at all ✅.** Renovation is a rare, expensive, irreversible purchase and the
governing emotion is fear. UK homeowners have lost billions to bad building work, there's no
licensing scheme — anyone can call themselves a builder — and about half of homeowners wrongly
assume builders are licensed. One in five delay work they need purely from anxiety about hiring
someone. Every real friction below is an unanswered fear, not a UX flaw.

---

## Stage 1 — Daydream

*Buyer's question: "What could this even look like — on a house like mine?"*

They're on Pinterest and Instagram months before they ever search for a builder. They aren't
judging beauty; they're trying to match. 🔵

**Check the gallery. Does each project state:**
- [ ] Property type and era (1930s three-bed semi)
- [ ] Town or borough
- [ ] Scope (side return, rear extension, full refurb)
- [ ] Roughly when it was done, and how long it took

**Absent looks like:** a grid of finished kitchens with no words anywhere.

**Why it matters ✅.** Under-35s are twice as likely as anyone else to judge a builder by their
website and branding — 20% say it matters versus 9% overall. That's the demographic entering
the market now.

**Knock-on 🔵.** No captions means no text on the page, so nothing for Google or Pinterest to
index. And nobody forwards a stranger's kitchen photo — people forward *"this is the same
layout as ours."*

---

## Stage 2 — Feasibility

*Buyer's question: "Am I even allowed to do this?"*

Loft conversions and permitted development, building regs for a side return, party wall
agreements. This is where searching actually starts.

**Check:**
- [ ] Any guidance content on planning permission or building regs
- [ ] Any page naming the towns or boroughs served
- [ ] Anything beyond Home / Services / Gallery / Contact

**Absent looks like:** a four-page site. Very common.

**Confidence 🟡.** The advice-site industry is enormous, so the demand plainly exists and is
being answered by someone other than the builder. I don't have search volumes to size it.

---

## Stage 3 — Budget ⚠️

*Buyer's question: "Is this a £40k thing or a £120k thing? Am I wasting my time?"*

**I previously called this the single biggest leak. I was wrong to.** That was my weighting,
not measured data. Handle it carefully.

**What's actually known:** hiding prices produces *more* form fills — curiosity converts —
but those leads turn into real jobs far less often. And some remodeling marketers hide prices
deliberately, to filter out people with a £5k budget for a £50k kitchen.

**Check:**
- [ ] Any price ranges or "from" bands by project type
- [ ] Any explainer on what drives cost up or down
- [ ] A cost guide or calculator
- [ ] The phrase "every project is unique — contact us for a quote"

**How to use it on a call.** Never assert this is losing him money — you can't evidence it and
he's run the business for 25 years. Use the traffic angle instead: cost searches happen in
volume and currently land on aggregators rather than on him. Then ask which side he's on:

> *"Of the enquiries you do get, roughly how many turn into jobs you actually want?"*

Starved of leads and drowning in bad ones are opposite problems with opposite fixes.

---

## Stage 4 — Vetting ✅

*Buyer's question: "Are these people real? Have they done my job, near me, recently?"*

Now they're building a shortlist and actively hunting for reasons to eliminate him. Standard
advice is to approach **five** builders to end up with three quotes.

**Check:**
- [ ] Case studies with location, scope, duration — dated
- [ ] Named team members with real faces
- [ ] Founding year, company number, physical address
- [ ] Own photography (not stock)
- [ ] Reviews with names and dates, or a live Google feed
- [ ] Accreditation badges — **TrustMark** (the only government-endorsed scheme), **FMB**,
      Which? Trusted Trader, NICEIC, Gas Safe
- [ ] Public liability cover stated

**Why this stage is strong ✅.** 33% of under-35 homeowners choose builders from online reviews
and 24% use comparison platforms — but only 8% check trade-body membership. The proof buyers
rely on is exactly the proof most sites don't publish.

**One correction to my earlier version 🔵.** "Stock photography is instantly fatal" was rhetoric.
Directionally sensible, not evidenced.

---

## Stage 5 — First contact 🟡

*Buyer's question: "What happens if I fill this in? Will I get hassled?"*

**Check, on mobile:**
- [ ] Form length — more than 5 fields is friction
- [ ] Does it demand budget before any trust exists
- [ ] Any statement of what happens next and how fast
- [ ] Phone number tappable, in the header, on every page
- [ ] WhatsApp option
- [ ] Any way to book a call directly

**On WhatsApp 🟡.** It genuinely is the default channel in UK trade — customers book, send
photos, and receive quotes through it. But every source I found sells WhatsApp software, so
trust the direction and not the percentages.

**On form length 🔵.** Standard CRO practice, no renovation-specific study.

---

## Stage 6 — Response ✅ ← your strongest angle

*Buyer's question: "Will they even call me back?"*

**I understated this originally.** Homeowners report contacting five recommended builders about
an extension and receiving **zero** replies. Another contacted nine across three rounds and had
one quote after a month. Builders themselves say they simply don't respond to jobs they don't
fancy. The FMB notes a detailed quote can take weeks to produce.

**Check:**
- [ ] A published process — consultation → survey → itemised quote → contract → build
- [ ] Any stated response-time commitment

**Why this is your best pitch 🔵.** A builder who merely *promises to reply within one working
day* differentiates against documented, industry-wide silence. It costs him one sentence.
And it isn't a criticism of him — it's a criticism of his competitors.

---

## Stage 7 — Quote comparison ✅

*Buyer's question: "Why is one quote nearly double? Who's lying to me?"*

Quotes genuinely arrive in incomparable formats — one industry expert describes requesting a
set format from every builder and receiving a different one from each. Buyers are warned that
the cheapest of three usually isn't actually cheapest, because the number means nothing until
you know what it covers.

**Check:**
- [ ] Anything explaining how his quotes are structured
- [ ] Inclusions and exclusions
- [ ] Provisional sums, variations, retentions explained

**Real terms buyers don't understand ✅:** 5% retention held until snagging is done; liquidated
damages of roughly £250–£500 per week for overruns; provisional sums for unforeseen works.

---

## Stage 8 — Commitment ✅

*Buyer's question: "What if it goes wrong?"*

**Check:**
- [ ] Payment schedule stated
- [ ] Contract type (JCT, FMB)
- [ ] Warranty or insurance-backed guarantee
- [ ] Dispute route

**Benchmarks you can quote ✅.** Industry-standard deposit is 10–15%; a 40–50% upfront demand is
a red flag; milestone-based stage payments beat time-based ones because they tie money to
progress.

**Why the fear is rational ✅.** Of homeowners who won court orders against builders who
disappeared, a quarter recovered only part of their money and one in ten got nothing.

## Scoring

Weight by revenue impact, not by count. Revised from my earlier version after checking the
evidence:

| Stage | Points | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 4 — Vetting | 24 | ✅ strongest evidence |
| 5 — First contact | 14 | 🟡 |
| 6 — Response & process | 12 | ✅ strongest evidence |
| 3 — Budget | 15 | ⚠️ contested |
| 7 — Quotes | 10 | ✅ |
| 8 — Commitment | 10 | ✅ |
| 2 — Feasibility | 9 | 🟡 |
| 1 — Daydream | 6 | 🔵 |

Absent scores nothing, Partial scores half, Answered scores full. N/A drops out of the
denominator — don't penalise a kitchen fitter for having no planning content.

**This weighting is still my judgment.** Run it past Seth before you build a pitch on it.

## Turning findings into calls

**Never open with a verdict.** "Your website has problems" puts them on the defensive and
you're arguing from second one.

**Open with the observation as a question.** True, specific, answerable, no criticism attached.

- Gallery: *"I was looking through your project photos and none of them say what the house was
  or where — is that on purpose?"*
- Response: *"Do you know how many builders never ring people back? I keep seeing homeowners
  say they contacted five and heard from none."*
- Pricing: *"I couldn't find any pricing guidance on the site — is that deliberate?"*

**Then diagnose. Don't prescribe.**
- *"Of the enquiries you get, how many turn into jobs you actually want?"*
- *"Where do most of them come from?"* — if the answer is word of mouth, that's your wedge
- *"How far out are you booked?"* — nobody is booked for a year; ask what happens after

**Have the site open when you dial.** The only advantage this whole approach has is that the
observation is real. If he asks "which photos?" and you fumble, it collapses.

---

## Two honest limits

Most of the strongest numbers come from the FMB, a trade body campaigning for builder
licensing. It benefits from the fear looking large — trust the direction more than the
decimals.

And nothing here is a controlled study of remodeler websites. The stage-by-stage *fixes* remain
reasoning. Your own call data will eventually be better evidence than any of this.
